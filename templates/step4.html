📊 Average Metric Scores

What it depicts: A bar chart of the mean scores for BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR, and Composite across all samples.
Why it’s needed: Provides a quick overview of each metric’s overall performance.
How to read: Taller bars = higher average score. Composite’s bar should be highest if it consistently aligns with human judgment.
Best vs. Worst: The peak bar shows which metric scores closest to perfect (1.00) on average; the shortest indicates the least reliable.

📈 Correlation with Human Judgments

What it depicts: Pearson’s correlation coefficient between each metric and human scores.
Why it’s needed: Measures how well each metric agrees with human evaluation.
How to read: Values range from -1 to +1—closer to +1 is better alignment. Composite should approach +1.
Best vs. Worst: The highest correlation (e.g., Composite ~0.9) shows the most human-like metric; the lowest (e.g., BLEU ~0.5) shows weakness.

📉 Score Variability (Standard Deviation)

What it depicts: Standard deviation of each metric’s scores across the dataset.
Why it’s needed: Indicates consistency—lower variability is preferable.
How to read: Shorter bars = more stable metric. Composite should have one of the shortest bars.
Best vs. Worst: The metric with the smallest SD is most predictable; the largest SD is least reliable.

🎯 Precision-Recall Curve

What it depicts: Precision vs. recall trade-off for identifying high-quality responses (human_score ≥ 0.7).
Why it’s needed: Evaluates how well a metric selects top responses without too many false positives.
How to read: Curve closer to the top-right corner is better. AUC (Area Under Curve) quantifies overall performance.
Best vs. Worst: The highest AUC indicates the best metric; the lowest AUC shows poor discrimination.

📐 Delta Histogram

What it depicts: Distribution of (Composite – average(BLEU,ROUGE,METEOR)) across all samples.
Why it’s needed: Shows how often, and by how much, Composite corrects or underperforms relative to traditional metrics.
How to read: Bars right of zero = Composite > traditional average; left of zero = Composite < average.
Best vs. Worst: A strong right-skew and positive mean delta indicate consistent Composite improvements.
